Teaching Philosophy

I believe that education is one of the most powerful forces in the world. From a utilitarian perspective, sharing knowledge is one of the highest-return things a person can do. It costs the giver little to nothing, and what one person learns can be passed on to others, so the benefit compounds exponentially. When I see someone reach that 'aha' moment, it fills me with an incomparable sense of fulfillment. So, from my perspective, there is literally negative cost; that is, I am intrinsically rewarded for teaching. I believe the existence of this positive feedback loop can be utilized for the betterment of humanity. Thus, I am driven to teach and to inspire the act of teaching itself.

I believe the mark of success in teaching is a student who can teach themselves. This follows the century-old adage: "Teach a man to fish and you feed him for a lifetime." My classes are 90% Socratic method and 10% direct instruction. I emphasize open dialogue and intensive questioning. My classes do not just focus on subject content, but also on how students go about learning it themselves. Students learn how to self-identify gaps in understanding and be resourceful in addressing them. This entire process starts with a question—"Do I understand this?" Then we break down what it means to 'understand.' How can we gauge our 'understanding'? What does it mean to 'learn'? Throughout our lessons, I show students how to use the internet, textbooks, and artificial intelligence to effectively support their learning. And to go beyond this, we ask, "How does one be resourceful?"

I also take emphasis on application. While theoretical knowledge is important, I believe that a deep understanding of the material comes from seeing it in action. This is why I include real-world examples and applications in all my teaching.

The effectiveness of these methods are reflected in my average rating of 4.95/5 from more than 200 college students on course evaluations.

In high school I received the President's Volunteer Service Award for leading a program that tutored kids of families in my city's transitional housing program.

My post-retirement dream is to start a K–12 school for the arts and sciences. I've been lucky enough to have a productive learning environment growing up, and I find deep purpose in creating more of these spaces. I've seen how much students grow when they take ownership of their learning, and when subjects like math and music are taught together instead of in isolation. I'd like to build a school around that.
